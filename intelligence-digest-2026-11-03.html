The big picture

Oil fell on peace talk, Indian shares rose on profits and tax hopes, and the bill for AI moved to lenders.

In short
  • Crude oil fell 7.6% on 2 November on talk of US–Iran peace, but US strikes on Iran went on the same night.
  • Indian shares had their best day since June on Reliance and strong profits, while foreign funds still bet on a fall in futures.
  • AI companies pay more to borrow, US tech fell into a correction, and gold and silver keep sliding.

Most of the news on 2 and 3 November came from 3 forces: the US–Iran war and oil, the cost of money in the US, and the bill for AI. Cheaper oil helped India most, and the AI sell-off hit South Korea far harder than India.

Oil fell on words, not on a deal

Brent crude dropped to $104.77 after President Donald Trump said he was in no rush to restart the war. The US struck Iran's Qeshm Island the same night, and Iran said on 3 November a deal was far off. Section 02 has the detail.

  • Stored fuel — diesel stocks are at a 35-year seasonal low in some markets.
  • Indian bonds — the 10-year government bond yield had its biggest one-day fall in 6 months as oil dropped.

So the fall rests on talk while fuel buffers stay thin. Cheaper crude lifted Indian bonds, Reliance and oil-using firms on the same day, which means these gains reverse together if talks fail.

Indian shares rallied, but foreign funds still hedge

Indian shares had their best day since June on 2 November. Reliance's record quarter, hopes of a tax cut for foreign investors and strong profits lined up. Sections 04 and 06 to 08 have the detail.

  • Profits — quarterly profit at 230 companies grew 33%, against 19% expected (Motilal Oswal).
  • Foreign funds — bought ₹3,115 cr of shares on 2 November, yet 89% of their index-futures positions are short (bets on a fall).

So the rally rests partly on foreign funds closing bets on a fall. With their ownership at a 14-year low, they have room to buy, which means oil and the tax decision now move Indian prices as much as results do.

The AI bill reaches lenders, and Asia

Anthropic's updated IPO filing shows sales rising fast but spending promises near $900bn. Lenders now ask AI borrowers for more interest. Section 09 has the detail.

  • US tech — the Nasdaq 100 fell into a correction (a fall of 10% from its peak) on 2 November.
  • South Korea — the Kospi fell about 4% on 3 November; 2 stocks make up about 60% of the index.

One view is that Indian shares are Asia's shelter from the AI sell-off, since few Indian listed firms depend on AI spending. The opposite view is that a deeper US sell-off pulls money out of all markets.

So memory-chip profits pile up in South Korea and Japan, while Indian phone sales under ₹20,000 fell over 30% on costlier memory (section 05). The AI build-out reaches Indian households as higher prices before it reaches Indian firms as orders.

Gold breaks down as India's money rules tighten

Gold fell below its 200-day average (the mean price of the last 200 days), and Indian silver funds have halved from their peak. Section 03 has the detail.

  • Smuggling — about 50 t of gold came in illegally in July–September after import duty rose to 15%.
  • RBI dollar book — a record of about $237bn promised in future dollar deals.
  • Money out — the RBI reportedly proposed a ₹50 lakh sub-limit on some money sent abroad.

Reading between the lines, the rules pull money in and slow it going out. So the RBI can hold the rupee steady while it owes dollars, but savers pay through duties, and smuggled gold sits outside the tax net. Falling gold prices also cut what gold lenders can lend per gram (section 06).

02

Oil, Iran and war: prices crash on peace talk while strikes go on

Crude had its biggest one-day fall since July, but the US struck Iran the same night and fuel stocks stay thin.

In short
  • Brent crude fell 7.6% to $104.77 on 2 November after Donald Trump said he was in no rush to restart the war with Iran.
  • Hours later the US hit Iran's Qeshm Island, one of a run of secret "limited" strikes.
  • Physical cargo premiums have almost vanished, but world fuel stocks, above all diesel, are very low.

The biggest one-day fall since July

US Secretary of State Marco Rubio said President Trump prefers diplomacy, and Tehran signalled a deal is possible. Brent (the global crude benchmark) and WTI (the US benchmark) each fell more than 7%, about $10 a barrel.

Talks by day, strikes by night

Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei gave President Masoud Pezeshkian powers to negotiate, with Qatar mediating. Yet The Wall Street Journal reported the US struck Qeshm Island on 2 November, one of several secret limited strikes, while Mr Trump spoke of a deal.

  • Strikes — explosions on Qeshm and near Bandar Lengeh; a US jet destroyed an IRGC (Revolutionary Guard) launcher after a missile was fired at US aircraft. Washington calls this defensive.
  • Iran's offer — spokesman Esmaeil Baqaei said Hormuz could reopen within 2 days if the US lifts its naval blockade of Iranian ports; Iran also wants over $12 bn of frozen assets freed.
  • Full list — Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi set 7 conditions, including sanctions removal, US troop withdrawal and war reparations (damages).
  • 3 November — an Iranian official told Reuters the sides are "far from a deal".
  • Shipping — UniCredit counted a record 35+ ship attacks in Gulf waters in October, yet Hormuz flows are back to at least 80% of pre-war.

Views split on what follows the 3 November US midterm elections. Mr Trump says he will not strike after the vote. The opposite side: Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu wants strikes right after it, and the US told Gulf states to prepare for a short, intense operation.

Reading between the lines, small strikes are already happening, so "no war" means no large campaign. Polymarket gives only 43% odds that the US ends the blockade by 31 December.

Russia hit, refiners paid

Ukrainian drones struck Kstovo, Russia's 4th-largest refinery, plus Yaroslavl and the Samara pumping station. Kazakhstan stopped flows on the Atyrau–Samara pipeline after a drone attack.

  • Marathon Petroleum — 3rd-quarter refining and marketing margin up 160% to $45.70 a barrel; extra $5 bn buyback.
  • Indian refiners — MRPL and Chennai Petroleum hit day highs on hopes of better GRMs (gross refining margins); Reliance's oil-to-chemicals unit had a record quarter.
  • Windfall tax — India raised its extra tax on export profits to ₹11.5 a litre for diesel and ₹11 for ATF (jet fuel) from 31 October.
  • Ryanair — its unhedged (not locked-in) 20% of jet fuel cost about $130 a barrel, double before; FY28 fuel bill seen up about €2 bn.

Reading between the lines, refiners profit from scarce diesel, not scarce crude, so cheaper crude does not end their gains. The windfall tax hands part of that gain to the government.

Europe competes with Asia for LNG (liquefied natural gas). Views split: Germany's regulator BNetzA says supply is secure, while storage group INES calls the low fill a serious warning.

03

The price of money: US yields slip, a Fed hike still waits, gold breaks down

US bond yields fell to a 2-month low on 2 November, but markets still expect a Fed rate rise in December, and gold and silver keep sliding.

In short
  • The US 10-year yield fell to 4.871%, a 2-month low, yet a Fed rate rise on 9 December is still expected.
  • Democrats are strong favourites to win both houses of Congress, the outcome JPMorgan links with slightly lower long-term yields.
  • Gold is far below its 200-day average and silver funds have halved, yet ₹900 cr of borrowed money still sits in one silver fund.

US bond yields fall, but a hike is still priced

US government bonds rallied on 2 November as oil and tech shares fell. When bond prices rise, the yield (the yearly return a new buyer locks in) falls. The 10-year yield ended at 4.871%, from about 4.92% earlier.

  • Short-term yields fell more — the 2-year to 10-year gap widened by 5 basis points (0.01% each) to 31, so the yield most tied to the Fed fell faster.
  • Bonds beat shares — the iShares 20+ Year Treasury Bond fund (TLT) gained 6.56% in a month; the Vanguard Total Stock Market fund lost 1.27%.
  • Fed path — 2 rate rises are priced over a year, taking the top of the Fed's range to 4.50%. Fed Chair Kevin Warsh says more tightening (rate rises) is likely needed.
  • Company credit — investment-grade bond spreads (extra yield over US government bonds) passed their 2026 widest; see section 09.

Reading between the lines, bond buyers bet the Fed does less than Warsh signals. JM Financial says foreign money returns to India for good only when US yields fall and oil eases; both did on 2 November.

Factories strong, prices hot; jobs and bond sales next

The ISM manufacturing index (a survey of US factory managers; above 50 means growth) beat forecasts in October. But only 34% of manager comments were positive, most citing the Iran war, tariffs and costs.

  • Inside the survey — the 10th month of growth; prices paid 75.2, rising for 25 months; factory jobs slowed to 52.4.
  • Jobs report, 6 November — consensus +62,000; Goldman Sachs and JPMorgan +75,000; September was +29,000.
  • Treasury bond sales, 4 November — dealers expect smaller 20-year and 30-year sales; Goldman Sachs sees $25 bn of 30-year bonds.

Views split. One view is that prices above 75 give the Fed reason to hike. The opposite view points to weak hiring and falling hike odds. So long-term yields also rest on Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent, who has shown he will push yields down, mainly by selling fewer 30-year bonds.

Midterms: markets expect a Democratic sweep

Americans vote on 3 November. Prediction markets put the chance of Democrats winning both houses near 80%, triple the level of a year earlier.

  • Polymarket — Democrats 95% for the House; Senate readings ranged from 72% to 77%. Kalshi: 72%.
  • Data centres — 60 of 72 governor candidates back at least one limit on data centres.

India's share market: Reliance leads the biggest rise since June

Foreign funds bought shares again, and a tax cut for them is under study.
